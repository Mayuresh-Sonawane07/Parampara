## 1. North India: Thathera Metal Craft (Punjab)

- **Element**: Traditional brass and copper craft of utensil making among the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru, Punjab, India.
- **UNESCO Inscription**: Inscribed in 2014 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (Nomination File No. 00845).
- **Official URL**: `https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-brass-and-copper-craft-of-utensil-making-among-the-thatheras-of-jandiala-guru-punjab-india-00845`
- **Government Document**: National List of Intangible Cultural Heritage, Sangeet Natak Akademi / Ministry of Culture, Govt of India (`https://indiaculture.gov.in/intangible-cultural-heritage`).
- **Verified Facts Grounded in Source**:
  - Artisan settlement occurred during the reign of Maharaja Ranjit Singh.
  - Materials: Copper, brass (alloy of copper and zinc), kansa (bronze), and pure tin (ranga) for kalai.
  - 8-stage making process: Ingot selection, pit furnace heating (bhatti), team sledgehammering, rim sinking, brazing (tanka), tamarind pulp (imli) and sand scouring, surface dimpling (kandhai), and tinning (kalai).
  - Dimpling strengthens annealed vessel walls through work-hardening while creating a signature sparkling finish.

## 2. South India: Toda Embroidery / Pukhoor (Tamil Nadu)

- **Element**: Toda Embroidery (Pukhoor / Poothkulli).
- **GI Registration**: Registered Geographical Indication No. 135 (Class 24 - Handicrafts), Certificate No. 187, registered in 2013 under the Geographical Indications of Goods Act, 1999.
- **Applicant Body**: Keystone Foundation & Toda Nalavaazvu Sangam.
- **Official URL**: `https://ipindia.gov.in` & `http://handicrafts.nic.in` (Development Commissioner - Handicrafts).
- **Verified Facts Grounded in Source**:
  - Practiced by the women of the indigenous pastoral Toda tribe in the Nilgiri Hills.
  - Ground fabric: Unbleached, loosely woven coarse white cotton cloth.
  - Thread: Red and black woollen yarns worked from the reverse side without stenciling.
  - Documented motifs: Pukhoor (flower), Modi (sacred buffalo horn / wild rose), Karthal (sacred kraal / architectural hut grid), and Enepukhoor (eight-pointed morning star).

## 3. East India: Chhau Dance (Eastern India)

- **Element**: Chhau dance.
- **UNESCO Inscription**: Inscribed in 2010 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (Nomination File No. 00337).
- **Official URL**: `https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chhau-dance-00337`
- **Government Document**: Sangeet Natak Akademi repository (`https://sangeetnatak.gov.in`).
- **Verified Facts Grounded in Source**:
  - Three distinct regional styles:
    1. **Seraikella Chhau (Jharkhand)**: Stylized papier-mâché pastel masks, lyrical Topkas and Upalayas, royal Bhanja patronage.
    2. **Purulia Chhau (West Bengal)**: Elaborate oversized feathered masks crafted in Charida village (Baghmundi), acrobatic 360° somersaults and high leaps.
    3. **Mayurbhanj Chhau (Odisha)**: **Maskless** (dancers perform bare-faced), disciplined martial Chalis and Dharans.
  - Performance context: Spring festival of Chaitra Parva.
  - Musical ensemble: Percussion driven by Dhol, Dhumsa, and Nagara; melodic line carried by Mohuri and Shehnai.

## 4. West India: Warli Painting (Maharashtra)

- **Element**: Warli Painting.
- **GI Registration**: Registered Geographical Indication No. 342 (Class 24 - Handicrafts), Certificate No. 211, registered March 31, 2014 by Adivasi Yuva Seva Sangh (AYUSH).
- **INTACH Cultural Mapping**: "Cultural Mapping of the Warli Community", INTACH Dahanu Chapter (2015), Convenor: Phiroza Tafti, Research: Pallavi Ganju.
- **Official URL**: `https://ipindia.gov.in` & `http://intach.org`.
- **Verified Facts Grounded in Source**:
  - Indigenous to the North Sahyadri range (Palghar and Thane districts).
  - Materials: Cow dung and red mud/geru background; white pigment from ground rice paste bound with natural babool gum; brush from chewed bamboo twig (bahru).
  - Geometry: Circle (sun/moon), triangle (mountains/human torso), square (chaukat / sacred space).
  - Documented Hotspots on Flagship Canvas:
    1. **The Tarpa Spiral Dance**: Men and women dancing counter-clockwise around the elder Tarpa wind player, symbolizing the cyclic rhythm of nature without beginning or end.
    2. **Mother Goddess Palaghata & Lagna Chauk**: Enshrined inside the sacred square by married women during weddings to invoke corn and fertility blessings.
    3. **Sacred Tree of Life**: Mahua tree connecting earth and sky, sheltering birds, peacocks, and bees in non-exploitative forest harmony.
    4. **Agricultural Cultivation**: Plowing with bullocks, winnowing, and grain pounding reflecting Kharif monsoon subsistence.
